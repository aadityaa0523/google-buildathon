# Cross-domain recommendation for Startup Buildathon India 2026

Research date: 13 November 2026. Scope: any domain; founder CVs were not used to exclude opportunities. This is a desk-research recommendation, not customer validation or a forecast of selection.

## Decision

**Lead: SceneAccess (working name), an Indian-language audio-description production and review tool for film accessibility.** Begin with Hindi, sell to accessibility/localisation studios, and test whether the product reduces professional editing time while preserving blind and low-vision viewers' understanding of a scene. Add another language only with native-language reviewers.

**Backup: pre-production specification checking for small garment exporters**, conditional on a factory supplying actual tech packs, sample revisions and inspection records. Its commercial outcome is clear, but direct competition and private-data dependence make it less attractive for the current two-day event.

The lead has the best combination *in this scan* of a comprehensible problem, a direct Shark Tank India connection, a substantive Google multimodal workflow, a bounded demonstration and a plausible business customer. This does not establish a new market or superior product. Its biggest weakness is direct AI competition; the proposed advantage must be measured.

## Event fit and timing

The official page requires a new AI product using Google AI and deployment on Google Cloud. It evaluates founder profile, product potential, market viability, architecture and Google technology use. Aspiring founders are eligible. The application asks for the idea, solution, architecture and deck; applications close **13 November 2026**, with city builds on 20–21 November. No public scoring weights or entrant count were verified. [Official event](https://startupbuildathon.com/)

Existing startups can be formidable competitors, but the application can provide concrete evidence: a sharply defined customer, an observable before/after, a credible delivery plan and transparent evaluation. Those are our proposed tactics, not additional published judging requirements. Founder relevance can come from problem investigation and co-design; a CV match alone is not validation.

## Lead problem: the story disappears when its visual information is missing

A film may show someone quietly swapping two envelopes while the dialogue discusses something unrelated. A viewer listening only to the soundtrack misses the action. Audio description supplies selected visual information during appropriate gaps in the soundtrack.

The proposed product helps a professional prepare that additional track: understand the scene, decide which visual events matter, describe them naturally in the target language, fit the narration into available time, and review the result. First customers to test are Indian accessibility and localisation studios; the producer or distributor ultimately purchasing the deliverable may differ from the tool user.

### Evidence, and the limits of that evidence

- **Existing distribution:** XL Cinema describes synchronised playback of AD tracks on viewers' devices. That demonstrates a delivery model; it does not establish a partnership or prove its suppliers need our software. [XL Cinema service terms](https://www.xlcinema.in/ad/terms-of-service.html)
- **Theatrical policy:** the March 2024 MIB guidelines set phased requirements for accessibility features, including AD, for theatrical feature films; their schedule covers national awards/festivals and other certified feature films. This is a reason to investigate production demand, not a guarantee of enforcement, spend or acceptance of AI output. [MIB theatrical guidelines, section 6](https://www.mib.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-12/o.m-dated-15.03.2024-regarding-guidelines-of-accessibilty-standards-accessible.pdf)
- **OTT policy:** February 2026 guidelines specify a 36-month implementation period for new content, encourage library accessibility on a best-effort basis and exempt certain categories. **Do not claim all OTT content has an immediate 2026 deadline.** A short demo clip is a technical example, not proof that standalone short videos are mandated customers. [MIB OTT guidelines, sections 4–6](https://mib.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-02/finalized-accessibility-guidelines-for-ott-platforms_0.pdf)
- **Language quality is a substantive research problem:** the 2026 Andha-Dhun study examines Hindi descriptions from eight films. It reports limitations in translated AD, including cultural adaptation, and argues for audience-appropriate descriptions. Its study supports investigating Hindi quality; it does not prove our system is better or establish willingness to pay. [Research paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06457)

### Proposed product, rather than a single generation prompt

1. Import an authorised video, dialogue timings, synopsis and optional approved character descriptions. Accept an existing human- or AI-written AD script too, so studios can test the review workflow without replacing their generation tools.
2. Build a scene timeline. Record visual events and the frame/time evidence behind each proposed description. Maintain an editor-approved character glossary; avoid revealing identities before the story does.
3. Generate concise Hindi descriptions and alternative wordings. Flag ambiguous events, unsupported motivation and uncertain identity for review.
4. Use measured audio intervals and rendered narration duration to schedule descriptions. Lock dialogue and editor-marked important sound cues; regenerate shorter wording or leave the item for the editor when it will not fit.
5. Let an editor approve text and timing with keyboard-accessible controls. Export an AD-only track, preview mix and editable timed script.

Character consistency, timing and multilingual generation are not inherently novel features. Their integration is useful only if the measured outcome beats an existing workflow. Our proposed initial advantage is lower **total human review and correction time for acceptable Hindi AD**, with no loss of viewer comprehension. That remains untested.

### Direct competitors

| Existing alternative | Verified product claim | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| [Klassifier](https://klassifier.com/audio-description/en/) | Generates multilingual AD, fits dialogue gaps, accepts context, supports editing and mixing | The general feature list is already sold; a similar UI is insufficient differentiation |
| [Visonic AI](https://visonicai.com/products/audio-description/) | Offers long-form timed AD, including Hindi, with advertised usage starting at $1/minute | Indian language support alone is not a gap; pricing must be compared with final reviewed quality and editor labour |
| [Synchrogen](https://synchrogen.app/) | Professional authoring, timing, transcription and voicing workflow | The alternative may be a productive human editor with tools, not manual work from scratch |
| XL Cinema and established AD production organisations | Existing playback and/or service workflows | Potential channel, customer or competitor depending on scope; no partnership is assumed |

Advertised pricing is evidence of an offer, not proof of transactions, adoption or willingness to buy our product. Public feature descriptions cannot establish relative output quality. Do not claim competitors fail Hindi without comparative tests.

### Google architecture and realistic scope

| Component | Proposed responsibility |
|---|---|
| Gemini video understanding | Scene/event extraction, draft descriptions and source-linked review suggestions |
| Google Cloud Text-to-Speech | Narration in a verified supported language/voice; measure output duration rather than assume word count predicts it |
| Cloud Run | API and batch media processing jobs; deterministic scheduling, validation and mixing code |
| Cloud Storage and Firestore | Proposed media storage and versioned job/review state |

Google documents multimodal video processing, timestamp queries and adjustable sampling; rapid events can be missed at default sampling. This requires clip-level checks and higher sampling where needed. Model timestamps alone should not be the final audio alignment. Voice features vary by model and language; check availability before implementation. [Gemini video docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/video-understanding), [Cloud TTS voices](https://docs.cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/docs/list-voices-and-types), [Cloud Run](https://cloud.google.com/run).

The two-day scope is a short-clip production and review workflow. Full-film continuity, studio mastering, secure enterprise delivery and certification acceptance are subsequent work, not demo claims. Google technology powers the central perception and language tasks; deterministic media processing supplies constraints and verification.

### Demonstration that could make the application stronger

Use a team-recorded or explicitly licensed 60–90 second scene containing a silent plot action, dialogue, a character transition and an important sound cue. Show:

- the original soundtrack and what visual information is unavailable;
- generated narration with its visual evidence and editable timeline;
- a deliberately overlong description rejected by the scheduler;
- an ambiguous identity held for review rather than confidently invented;
- the approved Hindi audio track and an exported script.

Use one live generation on a short segment and a clearly labelled preprocessed example for longer processing. A rehearsed clip demonstrates function, not general accuracy. Do not pretend that listening with eyes closed substitutes for evaluation by blind or low-vision people.

### Business and validation

**Proposed charging unit:** reviewed finished-content minute, or studio subscription with a usage allowance. Determine the actual rate after examining editor labour, buyer budgets and comparable tools. The useful margin calculation is revenue less model/speech/rendering cost, reviewer labour, support and acquisition cost. API cost alone is an inadequate unit-economics story.

**Initial route to customers:** accessibility/localisation studios with recurring film work. A pilot should fit their existing production deliverables. Do not depend on immediately selling to a major OTT platform or building a consumer audience.

**Defensibility hypothesis:** permissioned corrections and scene-linked quality benchmarks across languages, plus integration into studio delivery workflows. This is an asset we could build; it is not a moat we currently have.

**Proposed validation gates, not completed results:**

1. Two professional describers/localisation operators explain their current workflow, failure types and budgets; at least one agrees to assess a sample.
2. Blind/low-vision Hindi speakers help choose meaningful comprehension questions and review consented clips. A small pilot is directional, not a population-level effectiveness claim.
3. Compare the assisted workflow with a generic Gemini prompt and a direct commercial alternative where trial access is available. Use held-out clips, counterbalanced order and reviewers who did not author the scene where feasible.
4. Track editing minutes per final minute, critical visual omissions, invented events, character errors, dialogue/sound overlap, comprehension and total cost. Predefine which defects block delivery.
5. Seek a paid pilot or written buyer interest after a reviewable sample. No such commitment exists today.

The idea should be dropped or substantially revised if competitors deliver acceptable Hindi AD at equal or lower total cost, editors save no material time, user comprehension worsens, or potential buyers will not trial it. A policy announcement alone cannot rescue poor unit economics.

## Application draft — accurate at idea stage

> SceneAccess aims to help Indian accessibility studios produce accurate Hindi audio descriptions with less editing work. Blind and low-vision viewers can miss story-critical actions that are not conveyed by dialogue. Our proposed workflow uses Gemini to analyse scenes and draft descriptions, then combines an editable character glossary, source-linked review and measured audio scheduling to prepare narration around dialogue and important sound cues. Google Cloud will host processing and review, with Cloud Text-to-Speech for narration. We will benchmark editor time, critical visual errors, timing and viewer comprehension against existing workflows. Our first customer hypothesis is an accessibility or localisation studio paying per finished minute. The initial build will demonstrate a short-clip workflow; customer validation and comparative performance remain to be established.

Do not add revenue, partnerships, user results, accuracy percentages or cost savings until they exist. Before submission, the team should add a truthful founder motivation and the validation work it has actually completed.
