# Two agent infrastructure hypotheses after competitor checks

Research snapshot: 2026-11-13. Scope: agentic commerce and AI infrastructure. I rejected generic tool marketplaces, human task marketplaces, trust scores, observability, budget controls, and security wrappers. These are hypotheses, not proof of demand. The distinction below is the product gap visible in public specifications; buyers and willingness to pay still need validation.

## 1. Cross-provider transaction recovery for agent workflows

**Hypothesis.** Build a transaction recovery layer for agents that execute one user goal across multiple independent providers: book/charge/order across vendors, then detect partial completion, reconcile receipts and order state, and guide a user or agent through provider-specific cancellation, refund, or rebooking. The wedge is a failure-focused “saga” and evidence ledger across vendors, not another checkout or payment rail.

**Why this is a real seam.** Google’s AP2 already covers signed user intent, payment authorization, and a payment receipt; it expressly describes coordinated flight-plus-hotel purchasing. Stripe’s agentic commerce terms say seller fulfillment, returns, refunds, and exchanges remain the seller’s responsibility, and disclaim Stripe responsibility for transaction disputes and refunds. UCP standardizes checkout and order status/events, but describes order management rather than an atomic multi-business transaction. Its own repository has an open issue describing lack of post-placement order modifications (including partial cancellation and line-item changes). Together, the first-party docs establish that authorization/checkout are being standardized while cross-provider recovery and exception ownership remain distributed. Sources: [Google AP2 announcement](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-agents-to-payments-ap2-protocol), [Stripe agentic commerce terms](https://stripe.com/legal/agentic-commerce), [UCP order capability index](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/blob/main/docs/specification/shopping/order/index.md), [UCP issue #348](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/issues/348).

**Closest vendors / honest overlap.** AP2 is the closest standards layer for authorization and receipts; it is not a cross-merchant compensation or recovery service. UCP has checkout/order capabilities and policy snapshots, and should be treated as a likely future encroacher. Stripe provides payment lifecycle and reconciliation-ready metadata within its commerce/payment role, but its legal terms allocate seller fulfillment/refund responsibility to sellers and exclude Stripe from disputes/refunds. Coinbase CDP Bazaar’s x402 MCP proxy discovers paid endpoints and handles payment-required/verify/settle/retry; it does not claim to coordinate compensating actions across independently operated services. Sources: [AP2 specification](https://ap2-protocol.org/ap2/specification/), [UCP core concepts](https://github.com/Universal-Commerce-Protocol/ucp/blob/main/docs/documentation/core-concepts.md), [Coinbase Bazaar MCP docs](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/api-reference/v2/rest-api/x402-facilitator/bazaar-mcp-server).

**Buyer and budget evidence.** The buyer is an enterprise agent platform or travel/procurement automation vendor already paying for payment operations, dispute handling, and integration engineering. Stripe’s published terms and extensive commerce stack are evidence that companies are investing in agentic transaction infrastructure, but not evidence that they will buy this recovery product. The strongest early buyer is likely a platform whose agents transact with multiple suppliers and bears support cost when only part of a plan succeeds. That budget is adjacent and real (payment ops/customer support), while autonomous multi-vendor volume is still emerging. Do not price on speculative agent volume; sell a pilot against measurable exception labor and failed-order loss.

**Prototype / falsification.** Simulate a travel bundle: hotel succeeds, flight times out, duplicate retry risks a second charge. Normalize AP2 mandate/receipt, UCP order events, email/manual provider receipts into one timeline; detect ambiguous state; propose safe next action and preserve human approval for refund/cancel. Demo must show idempotency, partial completion, stale status, and a contradictory receipt. Interview 5 teams operating multi-provider booking/procurement agents. Kill if their existing orchestrator already has reliable cross-provider recovery, or they cannot quantify exception cost / will not own the integration.

**Assessment:** strongest of the two, but medium confidence. Specific gap after validation; go-to-market and data access are hard. Do not pitch it as “agent payments infrastructure.”

## 2. Agent service-chain change orders and deliverable settlement

**Hypothesis.** For workflows where one agent buys work from another API/service and then delegates part of that work onward, provide a machine-readable change-order and partial-settlement protocol: bind the accepted scope and artifact hashes to each paid unit, record scope changes, and calculate what is accepted/payable when downstream work is incomplete. Initial niche: B2B research/data enrichment or software delivery workflows using multiple paid agents/APIs.

**Why potentially distinct.** Existing agent commerce protocols mostly bind intent/cart/payment and expose order status. A service deliverable can change after work starts: source unavailable, schema mismatch, revised brief, or partial output. The buyer needs an auditable link from agreed acceptance criteria to a changed scope and a partial payment decision across vendor boundaries. This is not the human work marketplace angle; it concerns B2B agent/API service contracts and settlement evidence.

**Closest vendors / honest overlap.** Jobs for AI Agents already records buyer terms, acceptance criteria, private delivery, acceptance, and a verified Base USDC settlement receipt; dotsjobs supports acceptance criteria, buyer review, and rewards. Taskin similarly includes bounded task briefs, acceptance tests, explicit states, and direct payment terms. These substantially overlap any broad “acceptance layer,” so a horizontal acceptance test product is rejected. The remaining possible wedge is cross-provider change-order lineage/partial settlement for service chains, which none of these public descriptions claim to provide. AP2 receipts cover transaction proof but not work-scope changes. Sources: [Jobs for AI Agents lifecycle](https://jobsforaiagents.com/how-it-works/), [dotsjobs](https://dotsjobs.ai/), [Taskin](https://trytaskin.ai/), [AP2 specification](https://ap2-protocol.org/ap2/specification/).

**Buyer and budget evidence.** API/agent platform operators already spend on paid inference/tool calls and on reviewing vendor output; however, public evidence of a dedicated budget for cross-agent change-order settlement is weak. The likely budget owner is procurement/vendor management or the platform’s finance/operations team, not a model team. Start only where paid agents perform material, multi-step business work; low-cost tool calls cannot support this product’s integration overhead.

**Prototype / falsification.** Build a lightweight signed scope manifest, versioned change request, artifact-hash receipt, and partial acceptance/payment calculation over two mock service providers. Test with 5 agent platform or procurement operators and ask them to walk through the last real partial delivery/dispute, who absorbed the loss, and what system handled it. Kill if they rely on ordinary vendor contracts/manual review at low enough volume, or if marketplaces extend their existing settlement workflow to cover service chains.

**Assessment:** more speculative than hypothesis 1 and not yet validated as a startup. It survives only as a narrow experiment; do not build a generic acceptance marketplace or trust/reputation product.

## Recommendation

Prototype hypothesis 1 first because AP2/UCP/Stripe primary materials show a concrete seam between payment authorization and distributed fulfillment/refund responsibility, and the buyer can map it to existing support/payment-ops costs. Keep hypothesis 2 at interview-only stage: exact marketplace overlap is already significant, and evidence for a separate budget is thin. Neither is validated demand; a fast customer discovery pass should precede substantial implementation.

## Adversarial second pass: is hypothesis 1 a standalone company?

**Status: downgrade / not yet an independent startup thesis.** The first pass identified a gap between protocols, but orchestration primitives plus vertical provider APIs may already let a customer build the behavior. No public evidence found of an independent vendor selling agent-specific, cross-provider recovery as a standalone product. Do not treat “not in AP2/UCP” as proof of whitespace.

Temporal documents the Saga pattern directly: a multi-service workflow registers compensations, runs them in reverse order on failure, and uses durable workflows to retry/recover. Restate explicitly supports durable sagas with recoverable compensations and an operation trace. The 2025 PVLDB SagaLLM paper goes further into LLM planning, persistent memory, compensation, and independent validation; it discusses the exact flight/hotel inconsistency example. These are substantial overlaps at the orchestration/agent-architecture level, even though they do not ship travel-provider policy connectors. Sources: [Temporal Saga pattern](https://github.com/temporalio/documentation/blob/main/docs/design-patterns/saga-pattern.mdx), [Restate saga docs](https://github.com/restatedev/docs-restate/blob/main/docs/tour/workflows.mdx), [SagaLLM PVLDB paper](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol18/p4874-chang.pdf).

The travel provider layer also exposes real recovery operations. Duffel has cancellation quote/confirm APIs: the quote reports refund amount and destination before confirmation, and its order-change APIs cover exchanges. This means a competent customer using Duffel does not need a new connector to recover a Duffel flight booking; it needs workflow policy and state coordination. Amadeus offers APIs across flights, hotels, cars/transfers, and itinerary management, plus established refund tooling. Sources: [Duffel cancellation docs](https://duffel.com/docs/api/order-cancellations), [Duffel order changes](https://duffel.com/docs/api/order-changes), [Amadeus developer API guides](https://amadeus4dev.github.io/developer-guides/), [Amadeus Ticket Changer Refund](https://amadeus.com/documents/products/travel-sellers/ticket-changer/atc-refund-sales-sheet.pdf).

Payrails is a material adjacent incumbent: its own product page describes orchestration across 100+ PSPs and a joined lifecycle for routing, reconciliation, disputes, and refunds. It is payment-centric and does not claim travel fulfillment compensation, but weakens any broad “cross-provider transaction reconciliation” framing. Source: [Payrails platform](https://www.payrails.com/platform).

**What may remain:** a vertical, opinionated recovery product for *agent operators* that translates user intent and current provider policies into a safe recovery plan across independently owned business services, including human escalation when compensation is unavailable. Temporal/Restate provide execution primitives; Duffel/Amadeus provide travel-specific operations; Payrails covers payment lifecycle. A company would have to own the policy graph, connector maintenance, exception workflow, and measurable support outcome. That is not a protocol gap. It is a vertical product/integration thesis, with a hard data-access and liability burden.

**Revised go/no-go:** conduct interviews only; do not prototype the generic layer yet. Ask travel/procurement automation operators for actual partial-completion incidents, existing Temporal/Restate adoption, manual staff minutes, unrefunded losses, and who has authority to initiate cancellation. Proceed only if multiple operators show a repeatable unmet need across their actual providers and will share workflows/data for a paid pilot. Otherwise close hypothesis 1 as a feature for agent orchestration platforms or travel-management software, not an independent startup. The second hypothesis remains weaker and does not improve this conclusion.
