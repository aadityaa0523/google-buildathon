# India market red-team: four painful-workflow hypotheses

**Research date:** 13 November 2026. **Decision:** pursue only a narrow solar commissioning evidence product for PM-Surya Ghar residential installs; do not pitch a generic solar dashboard. This is a hypothesis for customer discovery, not validated willingness to pay. No interviews were conducted.

## Recommendation: commissioning evidence pack for residential rooftop solar

**Product hypothesis.** An independent solar quality auditor, lender/financing partner, housing society, or quality-conscious installer captures a PM-Surya Ghar installation before handover. The app organizes the contract, bill of materials, installed nameplates, safety/protection photos, commissioning documents, and operator-entered test readings against the scheme's published agreement/specification. It returns a traceable evidence pack with each requirement marked *observed / missing / needs qualified electrical test*, exact supporting evidence, and an issue list for correction or dispute. The initial segment should be 1–10 kW residential systems, with one state/DISCOM workflow.

The app should assess presence and legibility of evidence (e.g. nameplate/photo/doc consistency), not certify electrical safety from an image. It should not promise generation prediction or detect electrical faults from photos. Any electrical measurements are entered by the qualified inspector and compared with the applicable acceptance limit. The human inspector owns the final decision.

**Why the pain is credible.** The scheme is large and expanding: the MNRE/PIB reported 23.96 lakh households served as of 3 December 2025, against a one-crore target. The scheme guidelines require vendor inspection/evaluation during and after commissioning, at least 1% independent third-party assessment nationally, vendor ratings based partly on equipment, workmanship and service, and a consumer-vendor agreement covering installation and five-year comprehensive maintenance. The agreement says disputes between vendor and consumer do not involve MNRE or DISCOMs. This creates a concrete independent evidence and accountability job; it does not itself prove unresolved defect prevalence or willingness to pay. [PIB / MNRE scheme progress](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2200441&lang=2&reg=48) · [PM-Surya Ghar operational guidelines, inspection, rating and grievance clauses](https://www.mgvcl.com/Upload/solarpower/2024/MNRE%20OPERATIONAL%20GUIDELINE%20CFA%2007-06-24.pdf) (see pp. 15–16; agreement and technical specifications, pp. 21–27)

An official Goa release provides a real process-friction example: 346 prior-phase rooftop solar beneficiaries had long-pending central assistance that was resolved after government intervention. This shows that process discrepancies can affect real beneficiaries; it is not proof that commissioning evidence software would have resolved the issue. [PIB Goa release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2122226&lang=2&reg=3)

**Competition / direct overlap.** The National Portal already allows installation status, technical specifications, grievance handling, and pre/post-installation picture uploads. Scheme agencies already have inspections, ratings and a grievance process. Several OEM apps and Indian solar analytics companies sell performance monitoring; multi-inverter analytics is not whitespace. Do not compete as another generation dashboard, alert feed, O&M ticketing system, or government portal. The possible wedge is a portable, neutral evidence packet that maps what an inspector actually captured to the scheme/contract checklist across vendor brands, plus explicit human-reviewed gaps. The gap is an inference from the documented portal and vendor offerings, not confirmed absence of a competing product. [Scheme guidelines](https://www.mgvcl.com/Upload/solarpower/2024/MNRE%20OPERATIONAL%20GUIDELINE%20CFA%2007-06-24.pdf) · [MNRE portal grievance page](https://solarrooftop.pmsuryaghar.gov.in/customer/apply/grievanceRedressal) · [Intellisolar rooftop monitoring](https://intellisolar.in/solutions/solar-applications/rooftop-solar) · [EqualX solar monitoring](https://www.equalx.in/solar-monitoring)

**Payer and go/no-go.** Best first payer is likely a third-party inspection/commissioning firm or a multi-city installer with a cost of repeat visits and warranty rework; lender/RESCO or housing society is a secondary buyer hypothesis. Homeowners may be the beneficiary but likely face high customer-acquisition costs and episodic use. Before building beyond demo, interview 3 inspectors and 2 installers; ask to audit real redacted install packets, count missing evidence/revisits, and obtain a paid pilot commitment. Kill if existing portal/checklist plus technician photos already closes cases cheaply, if the product cannot reduce inspection/rework time, or if inspectors will not trust its evidence boundary.

**48-hour proof.** Use a small curated set of redacted install documents and staged equipment photos (mock data clearly labeled). Extract serial/nameplate text and document fields with Gemini multimodal; use structured output to populate evidence slots; deterministic rules compare captured fields against a versioned checklist and flag missing or conflicting evidence. Show clickable provenance from each finding to the original page/photo and an auditable PDF/JSON pack. Ask the user to confirm uncertain OCR and classify any electrical check as requiring an inspector. Test with seeded omissions/mismatches and report whether each is caught; don't claim field accuracy from staged samples. Google AI is in evidence extraction, multimodal association, and uncertainty summarization; rules decide compliance status. Cloud Run can host the thin API. This is a credible AI-core demo only if it visibly handles image+document evidence and grounds every result, not if it is a form with a chatbot.

## Why the other three do not win

### 1) Commercial utility/telecom invoice dispute leakage — reject as pitched

The underlying leakage is credible and buyers have budgets. But major providers already sell the core job: Wipro/Tangoe advertises technology expense management covering telecom invoice audit, recovery, inventory, and bill pay; Infosys BPM advertises a platform-agnostic cloud/telecom expense management service with invoice processing, contract validation and dispute resolution. These are direct product/service overlaps, not adjacent analogues. A narrow Indian utility-bill reconciliation wedge might still exist, but the present hypothesis does not identify a defensible underserved buyer, proprietary data advantage, or distribution path. An AI agent reading bills and contracts alone is not differentiation. [Wipro/Tangoe TEM](https://www.wipro.com/partners/tangoe/) · [Infosys BPM CTEMS](https://www.infosysbpm.com/industries/communication-service-providers/service-offerings/cloud-and-telecom-expense-management-solution.html)

**Verdict:** reject generic invoice audit / dispute automation. Reopen only with evidence from a sharply bounded buyer (for example, a mid-market operator facing a specific Indian tariff/contract format) and proof that incumbent TEM offerings fail them.

### 2) Reverse logistics / warranty claims, counterfeit or serial swap — reject direct overlap

This is a painful and money-linked workflow, but the proposed visual delivery/return comparison and serial/IMEI checks are already squarely addressed. Vefri describes itself as return-verification infrastructure for Indian D2C, comparing delivery and return visual records and specifically offering serial/IMEI/accessory checks for electronics. Shiptri advertises doorstep verification, photo/video evidence linked to AWB, condition grading and fraud profiling. DRISKO advertises return-abuse scoring including empty-box swaps and serial-returner behavior. Brand-side IMEI authenticity/warranty lookups also exist (e.g. realme). This is an unusually close overlap; “AI chain of custody” or an India-first label does not make it novel. [Vefri](https://www.vefri.io/about) · [Shiptri](https://shiptri.com/) · [DRISKO](https://drisko.ai/) · [realme India IMEI/warranty check](https://www.realme.com/in/support/phonecheck)

**Verdict:** reject this exact product. A distinct pre-warranty repair/parts adjudication workflow would need a separately evidenced customer and incumbency gap.

### 3) Rooftop solar installation / underperformance — generic monitoring rejected; commissioning evidence retained

Generic performance monitoring is already served by inverter portals, rooftop products, and Indian C&I/portfolio analytics firms, so an alert/dashboard pitch is a direct category overlap. The government portal, vendor-rating framework, inspection requirement and grievance process also mean an app for filing complaints is not a gap. The smaller commissioning evidence-packet idea above is a different workflow and has a stronger demo boundary; even that must prove it speeds paid inspections or reduces rework.

**Verdict:** select only the evidence/QA wedge, subject to customer discovery and careful limits on visual inspection claims.

### 4) Food adulteration / safety traceability — reject generic traceability and image-based chemical claims

The burden is real: official FSSAI data reported 44,421 non-conforming samples among 172,687 analyzed in FY 2022–23. But FSSAI runs FoSCoRIS, a national paperless inspection/sampling system with sample collection and traceability features. On the commercial side, AgNext advertises spectroscopy and sensors for chemical/physical commodity testing; Intello Labs advertises computer-vision grading for visible quality; UdyogYantra.AI advertises an AI/IoT/cloud food operations and traceability platform for processors/FPOs. A generic “farm-to-fork traceability” platform is crowded, and a phone camera cannot substantiate claims about chemical adulterants or invisible safety properties. AI can triage visible defects or paperwork and route samples, but chemical/safety conclusions need validated sensing or accredited lab results. [FSSAI samples and enforcement (PIB reply)](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1945166&lang=2&reg=48) · [FoSCoRIS, FSSAI](https://www.fssai.gov.in/business/about-foscoris) · [AgNext / NITI Frontier Tech](https://frontiertech.niti.gov.in/story/from-guesswork-to-guarantees-ai-driven-quality-control-in-indian-agriculture/) · [Intello Labs / Atal Innovation Mission](https://aim.gov.in/pdf/AI-Prenuers-Coffee-Table-Book-final.pdf) · [UdyogYantra.AI, Startup India Awards report](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/nsa5.0results/assets/File/National-Report_NSA%205.0.pdf)

**Verdict:** reject consumer adulteration camera checker and generic traceability. A narrower evidence-chain product for lab-tested lots might be viable only if a specific buyer proves a paperwork-to-lab-result reconciliation gap.

## Evidence versus inference

- **Directly sourced facts:** scheme scale, official inspections/vendor ratings/grievances and portal capabilities; named vendors' advertised product features; official FSSAI sample counts and FoSCoRIS scope.
- **Inference:** solar commissioning evidence remains underserved; a particular payer will buy; the evidence pack will reduce rework; food traceability is too crowded for every possible niche. These need interviews and a paid pilot, not more desk research.
- **Not claimed:** measured preventable defect rates, total addressable market, proprietary data access, confirmed Shark Tank appearance for a candidate beyond the secondary adjacency above, or AI-based electrical/chemical certification.
