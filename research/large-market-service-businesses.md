# India opportunity: operating software for commercial HVAC service contractors

**Recommendation.** Explore an India-first field operating and payments platform for independent commercial HVAC/refrigeration maintenance contractors, beginning with firms that maintain multiple sites for restaurants, hotels, hospitals, supermarkets, and small commercial-property operators. Sell to the contractor owner or service manager—not consumers and not technicians. The wedge is recurring maintenance plus breakdown execution: convert WhatsApp/phone requests into structured work orders, quote from asset/service history and a controlled parts catalog, dispatch a crew, record proof of work and customer sign-off, invoice, collect, and reconcile. This is a serious, large-market software hypothesis, not a validated business: willingness to pay and distribution still need field validation.

## Why this segment

Commercial HVAC has a better initial software buyer than solo home-repair technicians: one contractor can manage multiple technicians, recurring service agreements, costly equipment and spares, and invoices to business customers. A missed visit or delayed repair can be measured in repeat trips, technician utilization, time-to-invoice, collections, and contract renewal. This is an inference about the operating economics, not a claim that Indian contractors have already demonstrated a particular ROI. The product should make those outcomes visible in a before/after dashboard; payment capture and parts procurement are expansion levers only after the workflow earns trust.

India’s addressable universe is broad but must not be overstated. MoSPI’s ASUSE 2023–24 estimates 7.34 crore unincorporated establishments, including 3.04 crore in “other services,” but this category is far wider than HVAC contractors and the survey excludes incorporated companies and construction. It demonstrates a very large fragmented-services base, not a count of target buyers. The relevant target count needs to be built from NIC activity codes, OEM/dealer and contractor directories, and interviews; do not multiply the 3.04 crore figure by an invented SaaS price. ASUSE also reports only 26.7% of establishments used the internet, a reminder that onboarding, low-connectivity field use, and WhatsApp-first workflows are core distribution/product constraints, not edge cases. ## Evidence that the category can produce large software revenue

ServiceTitan is proof that trade-specific operating software can become a major software business, not proof of India demand or economics. Its FY2025 investor release reports $771.9 million total revenue, $739.5 million platform revenue, and $565.7 million subscription revenue (up from $441.5 million in FY2024). Usage revenue was another $173.8 million. This is a useful revenue model reference: subscriptions can be the base, with payment/usage monetization layered on top. ServiceTitan is predominantly US-focused; its filings say non-US customers were under 5% of revenue, so its figures should not be treated as India TAM. [ServiceTitan FY2025 results](https://investors.servicetitan.com/news-releases/news-release-details/servicetitan-announces-fiscal-fourth-quarter-and-full-year) · [ServiceTitan FY2026 10-K](https://investors.servicetitan.com/static-files/84e016f3-9a75-4199-ad42-b3babe1bb3dd)

Urban Company is a useful but different India comparator. Its official FY2024 summary reported ₹827 crore revenue and 30% growth, demonstrating that organized services can reach scale in India. But its consumer marketplace and service-professional network are not evidence that an independent B2B contractor SaaS model will sell. Its offer documents are valuable primary material for understanding the managed-network approach, unit economics, and category boundary. ## Competition and differentiation hypothesis

“AI for field service” is not differentiation. Zoho FSM already offers requests, estimates, work orders, service parts, dispatch, invoicing and payments, a field mobile app, workflow automation, APIs, WhatsApp messaging, and job costing. It has free and paid tiers and supports 25+ languages. A generic intake-to-quote assistant or a prettier technician app would compete with a capable horizontal suite and Zoho’s distribution. [Zoho FSM feature list](https://www.zoho.com/fsm/features.html) · [Zoho FSM pricing](https://www.zoho.com/fsm/pricing.html)

Test this narrower hypothesis: **contractor-owned asset and service history plus quote-to-cash workflow designed around Indian commercial HVAC operations can cut avoidable repeat visits and days from job completion to invoice/collection, while improving maintenance-contract renewal.** Potential defensibility is accumulated asset/parts compatibility and job-outcome data combined with practical adoption channels (OEM/dealer referrals, contractor associations, parts distributors, and accountants), not locality or AI alone. Keep the contractor’s customer relationship and existing workforce; avoid becoming a labor marketplace. The system must support mixed skill levels, owner-operated teams, multilingual/voice notes, offline job capture, photo evidence, GST-ready invoicing, UPI/bank reconciliation, and varied brand/model parts. Those are hypotheses for discovery, not established product requirements from market research.

## Commercial model and next proof

Charge a recurring per-business or per-dispatch-seat SaaS fee, with optional payment reconciliation/processing revenue where permitted and transparently priced. Do not make GMV or pass-through parts value the headline. Revenue should be software subscriptions plus earned payment/financial-service fees; procurement revenue should follow genuine repeat buying and reliable fulfillment, not an inflated addressable-spend calculation.

Before building the broad platform, interview and shadow 15–20 commercial HVAC contractors in one metro, including smaller firms that service several business sites. Request de-identified job logs, maintenance schedules, quote-to-invoice timing, repeat-visit reasons, parts sourcing process, payment aging, and current software/WhatsApp/spreadsheet stack. Pilot one tightly scoped workflow with 3–5 willing firms: complaint intake → dispatcher-reviewed work order → technician job sheet with asset/parts evidence → customer sign-off → invoice and payment status. Measure baseline and change in dispatch/admin minutes per job, first-visit resolution, completion-to-invoice time, overdue receivables, and monthly active use by field staff. A paid renewal is stronger evidence than positive interviews.

**Decision:** pursue discovery in commercial HVAC/refrigeration service contractors rather than a broad “all trades” launch. Proceed only if owners will pay for measurable cash-flow or utilization improvement and field technicians will consistently close jobs in the workflow. If the pain is primarily consumer lead generation or labor supply, that points toward a different, marketplace-shaped company and should not be smuggled into this SaaS thesis.
