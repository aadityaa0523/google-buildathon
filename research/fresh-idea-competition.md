# Fresh startup hypotheses: competition and demo reality

**Context:** 48-hour Google AI buildathon; team has computer vision, Android/embedded, FastAPI/React, agent evaluation, and Google AI/Cloud experience. Competitors below are sourced from first-party pages. No customer interviews were available, so buyer and willingness-to-pay remain hypotheses.

## 1. Phone scan to salvage sewing kits — conditional maybe

**Hypothesis.** A small tailor, costume studio, or upcycler photographs a flat irregular fabric remnant with a known-size reference. The app segments usable regions, asks for grain direction and defects, and packs a user-approved set of simple pattern pieces (pouches, patchwork panels, accessories) into the usable area. Output: a cutting map and reusable kit, not an industrial marker.

**Competitive reality.** Direct overlap is already strong: SeamScape Cutting Room says it camera-captures material, makes markers after capture, and nests patterns around irregular material shapes and defects. Aeronaut SiliconEye traces fabric/scrap outlines and nests patterns inside them. These target production tables/cutters, but directly disprove any novelty claim around camera-scan + irregular-remnant nesting. FABSCRAP collects/sorts pre-consumer textile scraps and sells reusable pieces; Reverse Resources traces industrial waste and recycling. TUKA SMARTmark and Optitex are established production-nesting systems. A defensible wedge would have to be different *customer and delivery economics* (e.g., consumer phone-only workflow, curated upcycling kits, no CAD/cutter), and even that is a hypothesis, not a unique feature. [SeamScape Cutting Room](https://seamscape.com/software/cutting) · [Aeronaut SiliconEye](https://www.aeronautautomation.com/technology/machine-vision/siliconeye) · [FABSCRAP reuse](https://fabscrap.org/shop-with-us) · [Reverse Resources platform](https://www.reverseresources.net/platform) · [TUKA SMARTmark](https://tukatech.com/products/smartmark) · [Optitex nesting case study](https://optitex.com/media/Optitex_Faurecia_case_study-EN.pdf)

**Buyer / kill criteria.** Likely buyer is an independent tailor, fashion school, maker space, or reuse shop; don't assume apparel factories will buy another nesting system. Kill if phone-only acquisition does not materially lower cost/setup for these users, or if they already handle scraps with a ruler and paper patterns. Validate customer and channel first: have 5 local tailors bring actual scraps and measure time-to-usable-cut-plan, yield, and payment intent. This is a go-to-market variation to test against installed commercial workflows, not a new nesting capability.

**48-hour proof.** Use one overhead photo with printed scale marker, OpenCV segmentation, and three rigid 2D templates. Show yield against manual baseline; let user rotate/lock grain and approve. Avoid claims of broad fabric recognition or production-grade yield. Android camera + deterministic nesting; Gemini structured JSON can propose a project template. Host FastAPI on Cloud Run. [Gemini structured outputs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/structured-output) · [Cloud Run](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs)

## 2. Bench-side circuit bring-up guide from schematic + evidence — strongest of three, narrowly scoped

**Hypothesis.** For student makerspaces and electronics teaching labs, turn a known breadboard circuit or simple PCB schematic into a guided bring-up flow: camera sees the current wiring, user confirms low-voltage DMM readings, system compares observations to expected nets, then proposes the next safe measurement and likely wiring mistakes with evidence. It is a lab tutor for *known, bounded circuits*, not a general repair oracle.

**Competitive reality.** Cadence inspectAR already overlays PCB design information on a physical board through mobile/webcam AR, exposes nets/components and supports attaching measurement data; it explicitly says it has no automated optical inspection for rotated parts. Flux is an AI hardware engineer that can design, simulate, and explain live schematics. Circuit Mind generates and verifies schematics against design rules/datasheets. Imula advertises natural-language workflows that connect AI to oscilloscopes, microcontrollers, power supplies, and collect instrument measurements (currently waitlist). So neither AI electronics support, visual overlays, nor equipment-driven measurement workflow is novel. The testable distinction is a much narrower user/job: novice-facing visual confirmation of breadboard wiring plus student-entered readings, with fault localization and a learning-oriented next test. This is not evidence of a moat; education market fit and actual diagnostic quality need validation. [inspectAR FAQ](https://resources.pcb.cadence.com/view-all/inspectar-faq) · [Flux learn electronics](https://docs.flux.ai/flux/Introduction/learn-electronics) · [Circuit Mind platform](https://www.circuitmind.io/platform) · [Imula](https://imula.io/)

**Buyer / kill criteria.** Best first buyer: university/college electronics lab, maker space, or STEM kit provider seeking lower TA load and better novice debugging. Kill if CV cannot reliably tell adjacent breadboard rows apart, students won't enter measurements, or instructors don't see reduced help time. Demo only isolated 3.3/5 V breadboard circuits and label the whole flow educational; never advise mains/high voltage. Test with 3 circuits, seeded faults, and blinded accuracy/measurement count against a fixed answer key.

**48-hour proof.** Hardcode an LED/resistor/MCU breadboard and a sensor circuit; use phone camera and colored jumpers. CV maps endpoints to a graph; readings are entered manually or from an existing USB/BLE meter. Rule-based graph/voltage checks diagnose; Gemini returns schema-constrained explanation and next measurement. React shows evidence and steps; Android captures; Cloud Run hosts FastAPI. Measure confusion matrix across seeded faults and whether the first suggested test distinguishes them. [NYU ITP electronics lab](https://itp.nyu.edu/physcomp/labs/electronics/) · [Gemini structured outputs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/structured-output) · [Cloud Run deploy](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/deploying)

## 3. STEM diagram to accessible editable graph + tactile print — reject as a 48-hour startup pitch

**Hypothesis.** Convert a STEM diagram into an editable graph with keyboard/audio navigation and tactile-friendly SVG/PDF. Potential buyers: schools, accessible-content publishers, transcription centers. Correctness and tactile interpretation require blind-user co-design and expert review.

**Competitive reality.** Mathpix OCRs STEM documents, math, tables and chemistry diagrams. Desmos supports keyboard, screen reader, Braille and tactile graph export. APH sells TactileView authoring software, Monarch tactile display and prepared science diagram sets. Kibo by Trestle Labs offers image descriptions/document access. More directly, research systems already cover key parts of the proposed workflow: TADA enables touch/audio exploration and node navigation for node-link diagrams; Chart4Blind converts line charts into accessible SVG, CSV, alt text, and tactile-compatible exports. Thus “semantic graph + audio + tactile export” is not a clean whitespace claim. A different customer, delivery, or production bottleneck would need discovery before pursuing it. Tactile graphics require adaptation, not a visual photocopy, so automatic conversion still has quality risk. [Mathpix OCR](https://docs.mathpix.com/) · [Desmos accessibility](https://www.desmos.com/accessibility) · [APH TactileView](https://www.aph.org/product/tactileview/) · [APH Monarch](https://www.aph.org/product/monarch/) · [Kibo](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.trestle.labs.kibo) · [TADA paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04502) · [Chart4Blind paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06693) · [Bristol tactile-image guidance](https://www.bristol.gov.uk/sensory-support-service/for-professionals/vision-support-for-professionals/modifications-and-resources/producing-tactile-images)

**Buyer / kill criteria.** District accessibility departments, publishers, or transcription services may pay. Kill if relationships/labels are wrong, blind learners reject the tactile rendering, or human adaptation costs the same. A simple cell-diagram demo cannot establish learning benefit, tactile quality, curriculum fit, or procurement viability.

## Recommendation

Advance **#2** only if the team can demonstrate the end-to-end loop on real breadboards and obtain quick educator/maker feedback. It best fits Android camera + embedded + agent evaluation, and the demo can be objectively scored on seeded faults. Its distinction from inspectAR and Imula is a *novice educational workflow and visual breadboard state*, not new AR, AI, or instrument integration; if this distinction does not matter to a lab buyer, kill it. Reject #1 as a novelty-led pitch: SeamScape and Aeronaut already solve camera-based irregular-material nesting; only an independently validated consumer/small-shop access channel could change that. Reject #3 for this event: multiple products and research systems already cover graph/diagram accessibility, and human validation remains essential. Of these, #2 is the most promising prototype, but none is validated as a startup; a real user conversation should precede a hard commitment.
