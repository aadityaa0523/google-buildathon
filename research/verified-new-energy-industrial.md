# Verified new opportunity hypotheses: energy, climate, industrial resource pooling

Research date: 13 November 2026. This is a competitive screen, not a market-size report. I treated a product doing the same user job as a kill even if its geography or branding differs. Absence from search results is not evidence of absence.

## Shortlist outcome

Two tempting concepts are already directly served and should be killed as startup wedges: (a) climate event alerts that identify supplier exposure and suggest alternates, and (b) an industrial water marketplace that matches treated-water supply with demand. A narrower, still-unproven opening remains in heat-safety operations for indoor process-heat factories: translating measured local heat and job-specific exposure into safe production/work-rest plans and evidence. This is a validation hypothesis, not a claim that no vendor exists.

## Candidate to validate: heat-safe production planning for process-heat factories

**Job.** A plant EHS/operations leader needs to decide, by work area and task, when to slow, rotate, rest, hydrate, ventilate, or reschedule crews during hot shifts, while maintaining output and leaving a defensible record. The proposed wedge is indoor process-heat settings (foundries, glass/rubber, bakeries, boiler rooms, mines), where outdoor weather alone does not capture radiant heat, PPE, workload, acclimatization, and local ventilation.

**Demand evidence.** India’s Ministry of Labour has directed employers/industries to consider rescheduling work, water, emergency response, rest and flexible schedules in heat conditions ([PIB / Ministry of Labour, 2023](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1917571&lang=2&reg=3)). NIOSH says indoor and outdoor hot-environment workers face heat stress and identifies workload, clothing/PPE, acclimatization, humidity, radiant heat and airflow among relevant factors ([CDC/NIOSH](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/topics/heatstress/default.html)). The UK HSE specifically names foundries, smelting, glass/rubber manufacturing, bakeries and boiler rooms among high-heat environments ([HSE](https://www.hse.gov.uk/Temperature/employer/heat-stress.htm)). These sources establish the hazard and operational response need, not willingness to pay.

**Closest products and what they do.**

- [Perry Weather work/rest cycles](https://perryweather.com/features/heat-stress-and-wbgt-monitoring/work-rest-cycles/) adjusts work/rest ratios based on real-time conditions and work intensity, provides three-day forecasting, and supports PPE/acclimatization thresholds. This is a close direct competitor to the core decision logic; its page emphasizes crews/job sites rather than factory-line integration.
- [HeatShield](https://heatshieldsystem.com/) provides WBGT monitoring, alerts, compliance records, worker training, and indoor sensor API integration; its published target sectors center on construction, landscaping, agriculture and field crews. It overlaps the compliance layer and could extend into plants.
- [HeatGuard](https://heatguard.ae/) advertises WBGT and worker-vitals monitoring, geofenced readiness checks, and alerts for worksites. This is close to monitoring and response, though its public page is construction-led.
- Standard workforce schedulers already manage production staffing and shifts (for example, [Indeavor](https://www.indeavor.com/)); they are adjacent and could absorb the feature.

**Potential unmet part (inference).** The potentially distinct product is not another WBGT alert. It is a site-specific operating layer for process-heat indoor plants that combines fixed/portable sensors with task intensity, PPE, worker acclimatization and production constraints, then proposes safe rotation/work-rest options and records what was actually executed. Public materials reviewed show the components split across heat monitoring tools and workforce scheduling tools; they do not establish that the integrated factory workflow is unserved. This inference needs customer discovery and a real competitor demo.

**Buyer / model / scale path.** Plant EHS or operations buys per facility, potentially with per-worker or sensor tiers and an annual contract; deploy first in one high-heat work area, then expand across sites/sectors. A durable large business would require repeatable deployments across foundries, metals, glass, ceramics, food processing, mining and other hot-work environments in multiple countries, plus measurable reductions in injury, downtime, absence, or compliance effort. No top-down TAM asserted.

**Google AI / Cloud prototype fit.** Build a one-workcell planning demo: sensor/weather feed + task/PPE/acclimatization rules → supervisor-ready shift and rotation recommendations, multilingual explanations, and timestamped action records. Use deterministic occupational thresholds and human approval; Gemini can help translate and explain plans, not decide safety thresholds. This is a prototype fit, not a moat.

**What would falsify the gap.** Kill or narrow if Perry, HeatShield, HeatGuard, or an incumbent EHS/industrial-control vendor already supports task/PPE-aware work-rest planning for indoor process plants; if plants rely on occupational hygienists/manual programs and see no recurring software budget; if they cannot provide trustworthy WBGT/task inputs; or if the safe schedule is operationally impossible to follow. First validation should be 10–15 interviews and one plant shadowing session, not a build-out.

## Killed: supplier climate disruption → alternate sourcing action system

**Hypothesis screened.** Match flood/drought/storm events to tiered supplier sites, materials and orders, then propose qualified alternate sources to keep production running.

**Demand signal.** A working paper using Indian firm and flood data reports that downstream buyers’ purchases/sales fall after supplier floods and that affected firms are unlikely to substitute suppliers ([NBER working paper](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w32218/revisions/w32218.rev2.pdf)). World Bank South Asia research reports fewer than 15% of surveyed firms changed markets, workforce, products or suppliers due to extreme-weather concerns and says contingency planning is infrequent ([World Bank report](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099031626155628132/pdf/P180518-85c81a61-f3d5-44c0-9667-2141fc6affde.pdf)). This is evidence of need, but low action rates may also imply weak willingness to pay.

**Exact competitor found: kill.** [Quibas](https://quibas.com/climate-risk-and-supply-continuity) explicitly links hazard signals to supplier sites, materials and open orders and shows an “alternative sourcing proposal” workflow. That is the same job, not merely adjacent climate-risk analytics. Broader incumbents include [Interos](https://www.interos.ai/request-contact), which offers real-time supply-chain visibility and risk insights, and supplier discovery/qualification vendors. The proposed product is therefore a clone unless it can establish a materially different buyer, data source, or closed-loop execution advantage; no such distinction was evidenced here. **Do not pursue as phrased.**

## Killed: local treated-water exchange for industrial clusters

**Hypothesis screened.** Coordinate nearby wastewater sources and industrial buyers around quality, quantity, treatment, logistics and dependable supply contracts.

**Demand signal.** India’s water-audit guidance explicitly calls for measuring water balance and quality needs and identifying recycle/reuse opportunities ([National Productivity Council](https://www.npcindia.gov.in/NPC/User/water_audit)). Government reporting describes treated-water reuse planning as including availability, distribution, demand-centre identification, institutional coordination and monitoring ([PIB / Ministry of Jal Shakti](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/erelcontent.aspx?lang=2&reg=48&relid=286151)).

**Exact competitors found: kill.** [NuWater India](https://www.nuwaterindia.com/) describes an e-commerce platform connecting wastewater plants with recycled-water users. The EU [Project Ô CEP](https://www.eu-project-o.eu/solutions/cep/) expressly describes a marketplace to connect water demand, flows, treatment and logistics, where users can buy, sell or trade resources. This is exactly the exchange job. Government-led treated-water reuse programs and physical pipeline/CETP operators are also substitutes. A new entrant would need an evidenced wedge in guarantees/financing/quality settlement that these platforms and operators do not provide; this screen did not establish one. **Do not pursue as phrased.**

## Energy flexibility: broad C&I aggregation is not a fresh wedge

India has active demand-flexibility policy work: Maharashtra’s official meeting minutes describe competitive aggregator selection, consumer enrollment, meters, dispatch, incentives and measurement/verification for a 500 MW solar-hour shifting program ([MERC minutes](https://merc.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/1st-Minutes-of-the-Meeting-DF-DSM-CC-1-2.pdf)). Existing vendors include [Ingro Energy](https://ingroenergy.com/), which advertises C&I battery optimization and aggregation of batteries for grid earnings, and [Tata Power + AutoGrid](https://www.tatapower.com/news-and-media/media-releases/tata-power-joins-hands-with-autogrid-to-expand-ai-enabled-smart-energy-management-system-in-mumbai), which announced behavioral demand response across residential, commercial and industrial consumers. These exact overlaps kill a generic “AI virtual power plant / demand-response aggregator” pitch. A new energy-flexibility hypothesis would need to target a specific asset class or settlement/regulatory bottleneck not served by the DISCOM aggregator and EMS operators; this search did not verify such a gap.

## Decision

Advance only the indoor process-heat operations hypothesis to discovery, with a deliberate competitor-demo gate. Kill supplier-climate response, industrial water exchange, and generic C&I flexibility aggregation as currently framed. The heat wedge has credible hazard evidence and adjacent tools, but no verified proof of an unserved market; willingness to pay and workflow differentiation are the central unknowns.
