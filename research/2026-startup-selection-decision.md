# India startup selection: latest-program research and decision

Research window: 13–14 November 2026. This brief supersedes earlier idea rankings in this folder for the current objective: a durable India business that can also enter the Google Buildathon and later funding competitions. It does not change the separate Nexus-B2A project files.

## Decision

**No candidate examined here yet passes the user's complete test: a differentiated product, demonstrable Indian demand, many reachable paying customers, attractive economics, and a credible large-company opportunity.** Public research supports several expensive problems, but not a validated startup opportunity merely because an adjacent company appeared on television.

The highest-priority remaining *validation hypothesis* is warranty-informed physical product redesign for Indian equipment/appliance manufacturers. Its underlying cost is evidenced in Indian filings; its differentiation, market size and willingness to pay remain unproven. New evidence of Tech Mahindra's offering materially weakens the broader concept. Do not pitch it as unique warranty AI or claim the software market equals warranty expenditure.

This is a decision to investigate a specific transaction, not a recommendation to spend months building. No customer interviews, signed pilots, engineering performance tests or revenue were obtained in this research pass.

## Selection standard

These are our commercial gates, not additional official Buildathon rules:

1. A recent, frequent or high-cost problem evidenced by actual transactions or operating records.
2. A named type of budget owner and a reason to buy now.
3. An advantage over current products, consultants, in-house teams and ordinary AI tools that can be measured.
4. Enough reachable buyers, at plausible prices, to support the user's growth ambition.
5. Delivery costs, onboarding, support and customer acquisition consistent with good margins.
6. A repeatable product whose next deployment requires substantially less bespoke work.
7. A technically honest Google-based demonstration, with the main customer outcome tested.

Competition is not automatically disqualifying. But an unsupported claim that an incumbent lacks one feature is insufficient: customer-specific integrations may already provide it. An empty search result is not proof of novelty.

## Remaining hypothesis: reduce the lifetime service cost of the next product revision

**Problem statement:** An equipment manufacturer's quality and engineering team must decide whether an expensive recurring field issue warrants a physical design change. Claim records alone do not tell it which feasible change will save more than it costs to manufacture, tool and qualify. Whether this decision is materially underserved at Indian midmarket OEMs still needs direct confirmation.

**Proposed customer:** one Indian appliance/equipment OEM, one product family, with service records tied to product revisions and usable CAD/BOM data. Initial sponsor: quality/reliability or engineering head. No particular OEM is confirmed accessible.

**Proposed product:** join field-service evidence to a revision, distinguish uncertain diagnoses, help engineers produce an editable design change, check specified geometric/manufacturing constraints, and compare conservative savings with implementation costs. The deliverable is an auditable engineering-change proposal. Engineers retain design and qualification authority.

An illustrative use case is making an expensive-to-access module replaceable without dismantling a larger assembly. This could reduce repair labour or parts cost without changing failure probability. It is not a verified problem at a named manufacturer.

**Why investigate India demand:** Voltas reports ₹73.48 crore of warranty provision utilization in FY2024–25 at consolidated group level. This establishes material warranty exposure; it is not appliance-specific spending, software budget, or our addressable market. [Voltas annual report](https://www.voltas.in/file-uploads/financial-snapshot/Annual-reports/VoltasAnnualReport2024-2025.pdf)

**Why it remains unqualified:** [Tech Mahindra Warranty-360](https://www.techmahindra.com/insights/views/balancing-warranty-costs-customer-loyalty/) already markets warranty analytics and quality feedback; [PTC Orbit](https://www.ptc.com/en/products/ptc-orbit) connects relevant enterprise information. CAD costing and repairability consulting also exist. The possible difference is a repeatable, constrained, costed design change grounded in field evidence. Public sources do not prove that difference is absent from competing implementations or valuable enough to buy separately. See [full commercial and competitor check](2026-warranty-redesign-commercial-check.md).

## Revenue and customer economics

Test a paid, bounded engineering study first. Offer recurring software only if buyers repeatedly use the same workflow and onboarding becomes economical. Proposed pricing is a hypothesis, not a researched market rate.

Illustrative arithmetic: 50 customers paying ₹6 lakh annually produces ₹3 crore annual revenue; approximately 167 such customers are needed for ₹10 crore. This says nothing about customer availability or profit. Engineering labour, CAD integration, model/cloud usage, security requirements, qualification support, sales and maintenance all consume revenue. We have not verified enough eligible Indian buyers to claim a huge market.

For future units made with a new revision:

```
Expected net benefit = future units ×
  [(old failure probability × old avoidable cost per failure)
   − (new failure probability × new avoidable cost per failure)
   − added manufacturing cost per unit]
  − one-time engineering/tooling/qualification/transition costs
```

Use matching warranty periods and uncertainty ranges. A repairability change may lower cost per failure without lowering failure probability. A new revision does not retroactively benefit the installed base; retrofits need their own costs and feasibility analysis. Cost-model output is not verified savings until supported by engineering and operating evidence.

## Buildathon compatibility

The [official site](https://startupbuildathon.com/) requires a new AI product using Google AI and deployment on Google Cloud. It lists founder profile, product potential, market viability, architecture, and depth of Google technology use among judging considerations. Aspiring founders can enter; the site does not establish a requirement for existing startup traction. The user-reported extension is accepted for planning.

Proposed implementation: Gemini interprets service notes/manuals and produces evidence-linked structured records; Google Cloud hosts the application and engineering jobs; a deterministic CAD/constraint engine performs explicit checks; the interface displays assumptions, original evidence and engineer approvals. [Gemini document processing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/document-processing), [Cloud Run](https://cloud.google.com/run/docs/overview/what-is-cloud-run).

This architecture fits the published technical requirements if implemented. No prototype has been deployed in this research task. Complete compliance with every portal-specific condition cannot be certified from the available public pages; see [earlier rules recheck](buildathon-rules-recheck.md). A technically compliant prototype can still fail the commercial selection test.

## Evidence needed before committing

These are proposed decision thresholds, not program requirements or established traction:

1. Interview five quality/engineering budget owners in the same product segment. Ask them to walk through their last costly field issue, current corrective-action process, tools, elapsed time, approvals and actual expenditure. Do not rely on hypothetical interest.
2. Obtain permissioned, anonymized data from at least two independent firms: a revision-linked claim sample, service procedure, relevant CAD/BOM, and one past engineering-change decision. A small sample tests data usability; it cannot estimate population failure rates reliably.
3. Reconstruct the past decision using only information available at the time. Compare against an engineer with existing tools and ordinary AI assistance. Measure evidence-link accuracy, data preparation effort, valid change proposals and decision time. Do not use hindsight as predictive proof.
4. Have qualified engineers reject unsafe/infeasible proposals and check actual disassembly or assembly constraints on a physical sample where appropriate. CAD checks alone do not certify reliability or safety.
5. Seek a paid pilot with a named budget owner and agreed acceptance criterion. A letter expressing interest is weaker evidence than a purchase.
6. Test a second deployment and measure onboarding/support effort. If the work stays bespoke, price and assess it as an engineering service rather than projecting software margins.

Stop this direction if buyers cannot share usable data, incumbents already solve the decision economically, the process is too infrequent, savings are consumed by tooling/qualification, or buyers will not pay for a pilot. Only after a paid repeatable outcome should the pitch claim a commercial advantage. Later funding applications should show customer references, delivery economics, repeat usage and a bottom-up buyer count; funding is never assured by a competition appearance.
